## Why

yog bl-96e0 changes the one-reader rule (REMOTE 5.1): an accepted advertisement ends whatever invocations read its certificate holds, so a restarted foot serves at once instead of waiting out its dead predecessor's claim. The read that was ended is answered with the ordinary invocations reply, no rows, and one optional key: superseded true (edition 29, no PROTOCOL bump).

A foot that ignores the key reads an empty hold, asks again, is refused by the new reader, waits, and on its next dial advertises — which ends the read that replaced it. Two processes bearing one certificate would then take the queue from each other in turn.

## What lands

- the vendored ledger and fixtures carry reply/invocations superseded at edition 29
- the decoder reads the key; absent reads false
- a superseded read ends that channel for good, with a sentence naming a newer activation of this certificate; it is never dialled again
- a refused read keeps its present meaning (a predecessor's claim on a server older than edition 29: wait and ask again)
- DESIGN 3.8 says so

## Done when

- a channel answered superseded is over, says why once, and makes no further dial (test against the in-tree server)
- the replay at every edition still decodes
