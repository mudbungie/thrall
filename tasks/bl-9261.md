**This is an engine-side defect, filed here because it was found driving the
foot and it is the foot that pays for it.** The remedy is in yog's mailbox
(`src/registry/mailbox/slots.rs`), not in this crate.

## What happens

An invocation posted while a foot's connection has died but the engine's
follow-class read is still parked is **marked taken by a thread that cannot
deliver it, and is then never redelivered**. It has no capture, and the asking
side reads that as *the far machine is still running it* until the slot's
hour-long TTL sweeps it.

## Proven both directions

- **Inside the window** — stop the foot, post an invocation about two seconds
  later, restart the foot. The foot comes up, re-presents its set, and serves
  new invocations immediately. The one posted during the window never arrives
  and never earns a capture, indefinitely.
- **Outside the window** — stop the foot, wait past the engine's hold, post the
  same invocation, restart the foot. It is delivered and answered normally.

The two runs differ in nothing but the wait, which is what makes the parked
read the cause rather than a guess at one.

## Why

`Mailbox::take` polls `drain` for up to `HOLD_WAITS * HOLD_TICK` — 240 looks
125 ms apart, thirty seconds — and a thread sleeping in that loop does not
learn that its peer's socket is gone. `drain` is a one-way flag:

    for slot in slots.live.values_mut() {
        if slot.client == client && !slot.taken {
            slot.taken = true;
            out.push(slot.invocation.clone());
        }
    }

`taken` is set at the moment the invocation is handed to the *answering code*,
not at the moment the client receives it. Nothing un-takes it, nothing
acknowledges it, and no later `take` will offer it again. So every path where
the connection dies between the drain and the client's `complete` consumes an
invocation silently.

## Why the window is routine, not exotic

Thirty seconds is long, and the events that land in it are ordinary: a foot
restarting under its supervisor, a network blip, an operator upgrading the box
— and every case where the foot itself dies mid-invocation, which the two
sibling balls make easy to reach.

## What a reader sees

Nothing. `capture` answers absent, and absent is documented as *still running*:

> `capture` is absent rather than empty while the far machine still runs it, so
> a reader never has to tell "not finished" from "finished saying nothing".

That sentence is load-bearing and correct, which is why a lost invocation is
indistinguishable from a slow one for the full hour.

## Shape of a fix (not prescribing)

`taken` wants to be a lease rather than a latch — set on hand-off, cleared if
the connection carrying it ends without a `complete`. The engine already knows
when a connection ends; presence is keyed on exactly that.

---

Fixed engine-side as yog bl-e658 (delivered to yog main): the mailbox's hand-off mark is a lease, not a latch. A slot the client was handed and the engine has no capture for is re-queued at that client's next follow-class read — the connection cannot be the lease's scope, since a foot drops it the moment the answer is read. The leg is at-least-once now and the invocation id is stable across a redelivery, so it is the idempotency key a foot may dedupe on. Recorded in yog REMOTE section 5.3.
