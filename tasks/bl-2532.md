`exec::tests::bounds::a_tool_that_leaves_a_helper_holding_the_pipes_still_answers` (bl-6c14) gives the whole capture a 2s wall-clock budget: a worker thread runs `execute` and the test body does `recv_timeout(2s)`.

The drive it guards is sound. Measured over three full instrumented suite runs on a loaded box, the capture arrived in 1503ms, 2018ms and 2112ms — bunched at the wall, and nowhere near the 5s the helper holds the pipes for. So the answer is never waiting on the stranger; what the budget actually measures is how long this test's own thread queues behind 164 others, most of which fork, under coverage instrumentation. Alone under the same instrumentation it answers in 0.07s.

The cost is not cosmetic: this is the close gate, so a busy box refuses every close in the repo on a red that is about the box rather than the tree. It cost three close attempts on bl-e95a.

The reframe: the claim is "the capture does not wait on a stranger holding the pipes", and a fixed wall-clock number is the wrong instrument for it — every such number is a bet on machine speed. State the claim as a RELATION instead: let the helper outlive the deadline handed to `execute`, so waiting on it yields a deterministic TIMED_OUT capture rather than a race, and put the budget far enough above the observed contention that a slower box changes nothing.

Same shape wherever else a bounds test names a wall-clock constant.
