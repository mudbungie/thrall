yog bl-278f records the measurement and ruling (read it with bl -C <the yog checkout> show bl-278f, and yog REMOTE §13.3–§13.4 once amended): on the phone the re-punch fired from exactly the port the landed call named and no SYN reached the engine's kernel — the engine's home NAT holds no mapping once the served stream has ended, and the engine sends no SYNs outside a call's window, so a client-only re-punch is one-sided. The ladder becomes: held line → direct address → RE-CALL (with cached presence: endpoints + the engine's punch port from the last read, write a fresh call, skip the presence walk, punch from the entry's port; a call that expires unanswered invalidates the presence cache so the next climb reads presence) → full rendezvous. The held connection's 25 s pings are the only thing that keeps a NAT mapping alive, so a dropped line is always a re-call, never a re-punch. Replace rung 3 accordingly, retire the re-punch lines in say.rs for the re-call lines ('re-call from cached presence — nonce N, k endpoint(s), a ack(s)'), keep the cached endpoints only as the re-call's input.
