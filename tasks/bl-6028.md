## What happens

A tool whose output exceeds the wire's frame limit does not earn a failed
capture — it **kills the foot**, and the invocation is never answered by
anything.

Driven with a tool that prints about 19.2 MB to stdout. The tool ran and exited
0. The foot then exited 1 with:

    thrall: <entry>: send: frame of 19200089 bytes exceeds the 16777216-byte limit

The invocation has no capture and never will. The process is gone.

## Why

`exec` reads the child's pipes with `read_to_end` — unbounded — transcodes the
whole thing and hands back a `Capture`. `run::answer` posts it, `channel::ask`
writes it, and `frame::write_bytes` refuses a body over `MAX_FRAME`:

    let len = match u32::try_from(body.len()) {
        Ok(len) if body.len() <= MAX_FRAME => len,
        _ => return Err(oversize(body.len())),
    };

That `Err` becomes the channel's sentence. `run::hold` returns it, `fan`
collects it, `serve` answers `Verdict::failed`. A transport refusal and a
capture too big to send are the same `Err` to every layer above the frame, and
only one of them is actually a dead channel.

## Why it matters

- The size is knowable at the moment of the failure. thrall is holding the
  bytes and `MAX_FRAME` is a constant in the same crate. It could answer the
  shape it already answers for a deadline — a truncated capture plus a
  `thrall:` note saying what was dropped and why — and keep the channel.
- Instead it takes down every invocation queued behind it, and by DESIGN §2 it
  never reconnects, so the box is dead to that engine until its supervisor acts.
- With more than one entry the other channels survive, but the failed one is
  gone for the process's whole remaining life.
- The invocation is unrecoverable even across a restart (see the sibling ball
  on invocations drained into a dead connection).

## The unbounded read is the same defect one step earlier

Before the frame limit is reached at all, thrall has already allocated the
child's entire output. A tool that prints without bound takes the foot out with
an allocation failure rather than a sentence. The frame limit is a wire fact;
what is missing is a local bound on what a capture may grow to, which is the
thing this component can actually enforce.

## Against the design

Same clause as the sibling ball — `exec.rs`: *"Every outcome is a capture ...
an invocation that earned no answer would be the hang the whole leg exists to
exclude."* An output size is exactly the kind of local fact a foot is supposed
to answer for.

## Repro

A tool entry whose command prints more than 16 MiB to stdout. Invoke it. The
foot exits; the invocation never completes.
